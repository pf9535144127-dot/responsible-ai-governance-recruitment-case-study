# AI Governance Policy – Portfolio Draft

**Organisation:** GlobalTech Services Ltd. (fictional)  
**Framework reference:** NIST AI RMF 1.0 – GOVERN  
**Document status:** Illustrative policy draft

## 1. Purpose

Establish accountability and minimum governance expectations for the responsible selection, development, procurement, deployment and use of AI systems across GlobalTech.

The policy aims to enable appropriate business value while identifying, assessing, treating and monitoring risks to individuals, the organisation and other stakeholders.

## 2. Scope

Applies to employees, contractors, business units and third parties involved in AI systems that GlobalTech develops, procures, configures, operates or uses on its behalf.

The policy covers AI systems across their lifecycle, including planning, procurement, design, testing, deployment, operation, material change, retirement and vendor exit.

## 3. Governance principles

- **Accountability:** Named business and system owners are responsible for the use and operation of AI systems.
- **Fairness:** Potential discriminatory outcomes and unjustified impacts are identified and addressed.
- **Transparency:** Users and affected stakeholders receive appropriate information about AI use and limitations.
- **Human oversight:** Human review is meaningful, appropriately informed and capable of challenging or overriding AI outputs.
- **Privacy:** Personal data is processed for defined, authorised purposes with suitable safeguards.
- **Security and resilience:** AI systems and associated data are protected against unauthorised access, misuse and disruption.
- **Reliability:** Performance is evaluated against the intended context and monitored over time.
- **Continuous improvement:** Risks, incidents, model changes and stakeholder feedback inform reassessment.

## 4. Governance roles

| Role | Core responsibility |
|---|---|
| Executive management | Set direction, risk appetite and oversight expectations |
| AI Governance Committee / authorised approval body | Review significant AI risks and make decisions within delegated authority |
| AI Governance Function | Maintain policy, inventory, assessment approach, oversight and escalation |
| Business owner | Define purpose, business outcomes, users and operational controls |
| AI/system owner and technical teams | Provide system documentation, testing, technical controls and change information |
| Legal, Privacy and Compliance | Assess applicable legal, regulatory and privacy obligations |
| Information Security | Assess security threats and safeguards |
| Procurement / Vendor Management | Conduct due diligence and maintain vendor and contractual controls |
| End users | Use AI within approved purposes, apply human judgement and report concerns |

## 5. Approval and risk acceptance

Approval authority must be proportionate to the use case, impact and residual risk.

- Lower-risk use cases may be approved by a delegated business owner after required checks.
- Use cases with material impacts require relevant specialist reviews and approval by an appropriately authorised senior body.
- High-impact use cases must not proceed while critical risks or mandatory legal requirements remain unresolved.
- The business owner, AI Governance Function and technical team have distinct responsibilities; risk acceptance must be made by the authorised role under the organisation's governance arrangements.

## 6. Lifecycle controls

AI systems must be recorded in an AI inventory and assessed before use. Assessments should cover purpose, stakeholders, data, foreseeable impacts, performance, privacy, security, vendor dependency, human oversight and monitoring.

Material changes must trigger reassessment. Incidents, complaints, unexpected outcomes and changes in law or context may also trigger reassessment or suspension.

## 7. Third-party AI

Third-party AI must undergo risk-based due diligence. Contracts should define permitted processing, security, confidentiality, subprocessor use, incident cooperation, audit evidence, model changes, data retention and exit obligations.

Vendor claims must be supported by evidence proportionate to the use case. Vendor intellectual property does not remove the need for sufficient transparency to assess risk.

## 8. Exceptions and review

Exceptions must be documented, justified, time-bound and approved by the appropriate authority. This policy should be reviewed periodically and after significant incidents, regulatory changes or material changes in AI use.

## 9. Implementation note

This is a portfolio policy draft for a fictional organisation. It requires tailoring, legal review, executive approval and operational procedures before adoption by a real organisation.
