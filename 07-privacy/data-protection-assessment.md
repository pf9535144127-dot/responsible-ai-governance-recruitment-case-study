# Data Privacy and Protection Assessment

**Use case:** AI recruitment screening and ranking  
**Scenario:** GlobalTech Services Ltd. (fictional)

## 1. Data categories

Potential candidate data includes:
- Identification and contact information.
- CV and employment history.
- Education, qualifications and certifications.
- Skills and professional experience.
- Application responses and recruitment workflow information.

The organisation must identify which data fields are actually collected, used, inferred, retained and shared.

## 2. Key privacy risks

| Risk | Potential consequence | Example control |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose expansion | Candidate data used beyond recruitment purpose | Defined purposes and restrictions on secondary use |
| Excessive collection | Unnecessary personal information processed | Data minimisation |
| Excessive retention | Data kept longer than justified | Retention schedule and deletion process |
| Unauthorised access | Candidate information exposed | Least privilege, access reviews and encryption |
| Vendor reuse | Candidate CVs used for vendor's own purposes | Contractual and technical restrictions |
| Cross-border processing | Data transferred without adequate assessment | Transfer and jurisdiction review |
| Inadequate transparency | Candidates do not understand relevant processing | Appropriate notices and transparency measures |

## 3. Scenario finding

The vendor stores CVs on its cloud platform for five years and has not clearly explained whether candidate data is used to train its own AI models. The contract does not explicitly prohibit independent reuse.

This creates a serious assurance gap. It does not, by itself, prove that misuse has occurred. GlobalTech should obtain facts, establish processing purposes and locations, review retention justification and strengthen safeguards before approval.

## 4. Required controls

- Identify the relevant organisation's role and the vendor's role for each processing activity.
- Document purposes and applicable legal basis or authority.
- Provide appropriate candidate transparency.
- Minimise data and restrict access.
- Define retention, deletion and backup handling.
- Assess vendor subprocessors and data locations.
- Assess international transfers where applicable.
- Establish security, incident and audit requirements.
- Review whether privacy impact assessment or other formal assessment is required under applicable law or organisational policy.

## 5. Vendor relationship and exit

At termination, processing should stop except where continued retention is legally required. Candidate data should be returned or securely deleted in accordance with the contract and applicable law. Exit evidence should address active copies, subprocessors, backups and relevant derived data or artifacts where applicable.

## 6. Legal framework caution

Potentially relevant frameworks may include India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act and Rules, UK GDPR, UAE privacy legislation and applicable US state or sectoral laws. Applicability and commencement dates must be checked for the actual organisation, processing and jurisdiction. This portfolio is not legal advice.

## 7. Assessment conclusion

The vendor's current privacy assurance is insufficient. The organisation should not approve production processing until the purpose, data use, retention, vendor role, contractual safeguards and applicable legal requirements are adequately assessed.
