# Responsible AI Governance: Recruitment AI Case Study

**Framework:** NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)  
**Scenario:** Fictional global IT services organisation using a third-party AI recruitment screening and ranking system  
**Project type:** Practical governance, risk and control portfolio  
**Status:** Illustrative case study; not a real deployment, audit or legal opinion

## Project overview

GlobalTech Services Ltd. (fictional) is considering a third-party AI tool that analyses candidate CVs, qualifications and experience and generates candidate rankings for HR recruiters. Recruiters retain responsibility for hiring decisions, but AI rankings may influence who receives further consideration.

This portfolio demonstrates how an organisation can apply the four NIST AI RMF functions:

| Function | Application in this project |
|---|---|
| GOVERN | Establish policies, accountability, oversight and approval |
| MAP | Understand intended use, stakeholders, context and potential impacts |
| MEASURE | Assess fairness, accuracy, privacy, security, transparency and reliability |
| MANAGE | Prioritise, treat, monitor and respond to AI risks |

## Key governance decision in the scenario

The recruitment AI use case is treated as **high impact for this illustrative internal assessment** because candidate rankings can materially influence access to employment opportunities, even where a human recruiter makes the final decision.

The proposed vendor is **not approved for production deployment at this stage**. The scenario identifies unresolved fairness, privacy, contractual and security evidence gaps. Deployment would require corrective action, validated evidence, residual-risk assessment and approval by the organisation's authorised decision-maker.

## Important interpretation notes

- NIST AI RMF is a voluntary risk-management framework. It does not prescribe a universal numerical risk score or a universal low/medium/high classification.
- The risk scores in this portfolio are illustrative internal assessment ratings, not NIST ratings or results of an actual independent audit.
- A human-in-the-loop label does not, by itself, demonstrate meaningful human oversight.
- Vendor certification or assurance reports are inputs to due diligence; they do not replace organisation-specific assessment.
- Legal obligations depend on the jurisdictions, entities, individuals and processing activities involved. Obtain qualified legal/privacy advice before real-world use.
